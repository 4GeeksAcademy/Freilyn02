About Me

Ricardo Guzman, better known as Ricardo Acordeon, is a renowned merengue tipico accordionist from Santiago de los Caballeros, Dominican Republic. He is known throughout the genre for his fast instrumental solos, for pioneering the fusion of merengue tipico with styles such as jazz, and for his rivalry with fellow accordionists Geovanny Polanco and Kerube Ortiz, leader of the merengue tipico band Kerubanda. While the latter two are somewhat more traditional in musical style, Ricardo Acordeon is more experimental and has included instruments such as trombone, trumpet, and Wurlitzer piano in his repertoire, along with the accordion, tambora, guira, conga, electric bass, and the standard saxophones of contemporary merengue tipico.

Biography

Ricardo began playing the accordion at an early age and gave his first public performance at the age of 5. He later appeared on several children's television programs broadcast in the Dominican Republic. Later on, he studied jazz at the Berklee College of Music in the United States and went on to incorporate jazz elements into his improvisation techniques and albums. Beyond fusion, he is also known for his interpretations of traditional merengue tipico and salsa standards such as "Juanita Morel," "El Estrujao," "Cualquiera Llora (Táctico Llorando)," and "La Vida es un Carnaval." His interpretations of American music include "Mountain Dance" originally performed by Dave Grusin and "Twist and Shout" by The Beatles.
